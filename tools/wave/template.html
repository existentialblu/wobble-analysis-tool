    How WAVE works

    Strength, duration, timing

    The amplitude envelope measures how the size of faster ventilation waves changes. Packet duration and repeat spacing are separate questions. A high quiet-to-active ratio can occur even when absolute waves are small.

    Same signal pipeline

    Third-order Butterworth bandpass, forward/backward filtering, reflected-padding Hilbert magnitude divided by √2, and centered smoothing. Default 120-second smoothing uses 61 two-second samples (122 s). The local baseline uses 302 s.

    Data and quality

    Device MV is preferred when PLD is present. BRP-only data use positive airflow averaged in two-second bins and smoothed over 14 s. The two sources have different amplitudes and remain labeled. Five-minute edge trims and leak/invalid buffers never bridge sessions.

    Longitudinal summaries

    Sessions within the chosen gap are grouped, with an 18-hour maximum span and separate ventilation sources. Groups under three recorded hours are labeled Nap. Period amplitudes are usable-time-weighted session medians, not pooled medians. Calendar trends use centered Gaussian weighting over the selected 7-, 14-, or 28-day window (standard deviation one quarter of the window). They use the displayed periods, keep sources separate, and break at gaps over 10 days.

    Controls and interpretation

    Controls use a 90-minute block with three minutes trimmed from each envelope end. Slow power covers 3–30-minute periods; repeat peaks search 5–25 minutes. IAAFT uses up to 100 iterations and reports spectral mismatch. Benjamini–Hochberg q values correct each metric across the current run’s blocks separately by method and source. Quick runs have coarse p-value resolution.

    Privacy and limits

    No analytics, accounts, servers, uploads, external fonts or third-party libraries. Data remain in memory until cleared; saving is explicit. Recording time is not scored sleep. No EEG, CO₂, effort or confirmed arousals are measured. Experimental, not a medical device.
